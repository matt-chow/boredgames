# The five expansions: player guide

Five optional sets, 69 cards in all, for Kingdom & Commerce. Each set is off by default: the host turns sets on in the
game setup screen, and the catalogue's set filter shows each one. They mix freely with Core, Crown Decrees and the earlier
expansions. None of them needs Crown Projects, so every card works on a table with **Enable Crown Projects** switched off.

Card text below is exactly what the game shows. The design reasons, the revisions made in review and the engine notes are
in [FIVE_EXPANSION_MANIFEST.md](FIVE_EXPANSION_MANIFEST.md). Rule IDs and tests are listed in
[CLOCKWORK.md](../CLOCKWORK.md#edition-5-five-expansions-and-the-crown-projects-switch-kc5).

## Playing without Crown Projects

The setup screen's **Enable Crown Projects** switch is on by default. Turned off:

- No Crown Projects are dealt, and nothing can be funded or claimed.
- The 8 market cards that only work with Projects are left out of the market and can't be chosen.
- The game ends when the Grand Duchy pile is empty, or when 3 market piles are empty (4 with 5 or 6 players).
- The round still finishes, so everyone ends on the same number of turns.
- The in-game guide for that table leaves Projects out.

Extra Buys lose one use with Projects off, since a spare Buy can no longer fund a stage.

## New words

Nothing in these sets adds a resource, phase or card type. A few phrases are new:

- **Choose an opponent … they choose:** the opponent you name makes the choice, and its effects still go to whoever the card says.
- **At the start of your next turn:** an Establishment's later effect. The card stays in play until then.
- **To your hand:** a gained card goes into your hand instead of your discard pile, so you can use it this turn.
- **Play it twice:** the card resolves fully, then again. It is still one card.

## The Merchant Republics (14 cards)

Currency economy, extra Buys, cheaper Currency and deals with an opponent.

- Silver and Gold matter more here. Fondaco, Bookkeeper and Ledger Hall all reward Currency, and Ledger Hall never counts Bronze.
- Partnership lets the opponent pick the deal, and one of its deals gives them a Silver. Expect the one that suits them.
- Underwriter gains you a Silver when anyone else buys a card costing 6 or more. At a big table, each holder is asked in turn.
- Gold Florin pays double in a turn with no Actions: a pure-money turn.

## Houses of Influence (13 cards)

Court politics: opponents choose your reward, Influence sinks, measured attacks and reactions.

- Influence has more uses: Antechamber, Bribed Magistrate, Whispered Promise, Purchased Office and Bourse all spend it.
- Several cards hand Influence to an opponent (Family Crest, Palace Intrigue). Influence above the cap of 8 is lost.
- Signoria lets an opponent choose your reward. Both options are good; you get the one they fear less.
- Household Guard and Counter-Intrigue answer Attacks from hand. Bribed Magistrate only hits hands of 5 or more.

## Masters & Apprentices (15 cards)

Action chains, playing a card twice, upgrades, thinning and draw.

- Master’s Hand plays an Action costing 4 or less twice. Whatever the two plays give, you keep at most +1 Influence from them.
- Promotion and Atelier upgrade a card; Sweeper, Guild Diploma and Schoolroom thin your deck.
- Bottega rewards long turns: it pays +1 Coin as your third Action or later.
- Maestro and Patternmaker work best with a good Action already in hand or in play.

## Ports & Provinces (14 cards)

Trading, gaining to hand, setting cards aside, Establishments and flexible buying.

- Five Establishments (Harbour Pilot, Ferry Landing, Bonded Store, Grain Hoy, Galleass) do something now and again at the start of your next turn. Cargo Manifest counts them.
- Bonded Store sets up to 2 Currency aside. Opponents see how many, never which.
- Chandler, Barter Post and Grain Hoy put cards straight into your hand, so they help this turn.
- Trade Agreement asks an opponent to discard too; both of you draw 2 if they agree.

## Glory of the Realm (13 cards)

Renown cards with uses, Renown that counts Renown, and endgame timing.

- Renown cards in hand stop being dead weight: Tenant Farmer, Banner Bearer, Laurel Wreath and Grand Muster all use them.
- Chivalric Order counts your Renown cards, itself included; Memorial Statue pays for low piles when you gain it.
- Victory Parade makes Homesteads and Noble Estates cheaper, never the Grand Duchy.
- Royal Jubilee’s bonus starts once the Grand Duchy pile is down to half its starting size: 4, 6, 6, 7 and 9 cards at 2 to 6 players.
